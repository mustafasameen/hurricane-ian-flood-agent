Question: How severe was coastal flooding in Lee County, Florida, during Hurricane Ian (September 27-30, 2022), and what were the potential implications for local transportation infrastructure?

Coastal flooding during Hurricane Ian in Lee County, Florida, was catastrophic and highly variable. At the Fort Myers gauge, the peak water level reached 7.26 feet above MHHW at 6:30 PM on September 28, 2022. These are current NOAA NWS thresholds applied retrospectively to the 2022 event. The gauge exceeded the major flood threshold of 5.0 feet for 7.5 hours, moderate threshold of 3.5 feet for 13.7 hours, and minor threshold of 2.0 feet for 20.3 hours. The maximum storm residual was 7.25 feet when the predicted astronomical tide was near zero. No observations were missing from the gauge record.

One gauge does not represent flooding across all of Lee County. USGS surveyed high-water marks within 5 km of the gauge had a median elevation of 7.5 feet above NAVD88, close to the gauge peak of 7.54 feet above NAVD88. However, marks at Fort Myers Beach showed a median of 12.3 feet and marks at Sanibel and Captiva showed a median of 11.1 feet. The National Hurricane Center report states that roads and bridges to Sanibel and Pine Islands were washed away. Such extreme levels would have potentially inundated low-lying roadways and rendered causeways impassable. Water surface elevation is the level at the gauge above a datum, storm residual is observed minus predicted, and inundation depth above ground is depth of standing water at specific locations that the gauge cannot measure.
