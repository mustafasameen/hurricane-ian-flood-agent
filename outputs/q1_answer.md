Question: How severe was coastal flooding in Lee County, Florida, during Hurricane Ian?

Coastal flooding in Lee County, Florida, during Hurricane Ian was catastrophic. At the Fort Myers tide gauge, water levels peaked at 7.26 feet above MHHW at 1830 local time on September 28, 2022. This surpassed all three NWS coastal flood thresholds that NOAA publishes now, applied retrospectively to the 2022 event. Water levels exceeded the NWS major threshold of 5.0 feet for 7.5 hours, the moderate threshold of 3.5 feet for 13.7 hours, and the minor threshold of 2.0 feet for 20.3 hours. The maximum storm residual reached 7.25 feet while the predicted astronomical tide was near zero. The gauge record is complete with no missing observations.

The Fort Myers gauge does not represent the full severity across Lee County. USGS surveys show marks within 5 kilometers of the gauge had a median elevation of 7.5 feet above NAVD88, close to the gauge peak of 7.54 feet. However, countywide the median was 9.1 feet with a maximum of 13.8 feet. Fort Myers Beach had a median of 12.3 feet and Sanibel and Captiva 11.1 feet above NAVD88. The National Hurricane Center report documents that roads and bridges to Sanibel and Pine Islands were washed away. These extreme elevations would potentially have overwhelmed low-lying transportation infrastructure. Water-surface elevation is the level at the gauge, storm residual is observed minus predicted tide, and inundation depth above ground is depth at a place that the gauge cannot measure.
