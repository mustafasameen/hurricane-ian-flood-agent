Question: Can the Fort Myers tide-gauge observations tell us how deep flooding was in Fort Myers Beach or Sanibel Island? Why or why not?

No, the Fort Myers tide gauge observations cannot tell us how deep flooding was in Fort Myers Beach or Sanibel Island. The gauge measures water-surface elevation at one location, not inundation depth above ground elsewhere.

The gauge peak was 7.26 feet above MHHW at 1830 local time on September 28, or 7.54 feet above NAVD88. USGS surveyed high-water marks show that peak water-surface elevations varied widely across Lee County. In Fort Myers Beach the median mark was 12.3 feet above NAVD88, range 11.6 to 13.8 feet, all 23 marks exceeding the gauge peak by a median of 4.76 feet. On Sanibel and Captiva the median was 11.1 feet above NAVD88, range 4.59 to 13.11 feet, with 52 of 56 marks higher than the gauge. Even within five kilometers of the gauge, 48 marks ranged from 6.5 to 8.5 feet above NAVD88. Water-surface elevation varies because of bathymetry, wave setup and coastline geometry.

Inundation depth above ground depends on both water-surface elevation and local ground elevation. The gauge has no information about ground elevations. The USGS marks show Fort Myers Beach had a median depth above ground of 6.82 feet, Sanibel and Captiva 6.3 feet, and the gauge vicinity 1.9 feet. Water-surface elevation, storm residual (observed minus predicted, 7.25 feet at the gauge peak) and inundation depth above ground are three distinct quantities. The gauge cannot measure depths on streets, at buildings or on the barrier islands.
